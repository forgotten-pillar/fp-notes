**Introduction**

Recently, a video presentation by Brother Scott R. [Last Name, if known, otherwise omit] has circulated, offering a perspective on the historical development of the doctrine of the Godhead within the Seventh-day Adventist Church. The presentation traces a path from the acknowledged anti-Trinitarian views of some early pioneers to the eventual adoption of the Trinitarian understanding reflected in our current Fundamental Beliefs. While Brother Scott utilizes historical documents and familiar Ellen G. White quotations to build his case for a legitimate, progressive development of doctrine, a closer examination, particularly through the lens of the research compiled in "The Forgotten Pillar," reveals significant discrepancies and necessitates a more thorough, contextual analysis.

The truth, especially concerning the nature of God and the foundations of our faith, often lies in the details. Misinterpretations, even unintentional ones, can arise when historical context is overlooked or when specific theological terms are redefined across generations. This article aims to offer a constructive critique of the video's narrative, not out of contention, but out of a deep desire for historical accuracy and faithfulness to the "old landmarks" established by God Himself through His word and the prophetic gift entrusted to this movement. We will utilize the evidence presented in "The Forgotten Pillar" to highlight areas where the video's interpretation appears to diverge from the documented historical and theological realities of early Adventism.

**1. The Centrality and Meaning of the "Personality of God"**

Brother Scott's presentation acknowledges the initial anti-Trinitarian leanings of figures like James White and Joseph Bates, often framing these views as "baggage" or early errors that were eventually corrected through progressive light. However, "The Forgotten Pillar" argues compellingly that this understanding minimizes the profound theological significance of what the pioneers termed the **"Personality of God."**

For the pioneers, this was not a vague concept but a foundational **pillar of faith**, intrinsically linked to the Sanctuary message. It specifically referred to the biblical truth that God the Father is a **literal, tangible, personal Being, possessing a body and form**, dwelling in a specific location – the heavenly sanctuary. His omnipresence was understood not as a bodily diffusion throughout space, but through the agency of His representative, the Holy Spirit.

> "That there is one God, **a personal, spiritual being**, the creator of all things... and **everywhere present by his representative, the Holy Spirit**." (Fundamental Principles 1889, I; Forgotten Pillar, p. 25, 75, 289)

> "What is God? He is **material, organized intelligence, possessing both body and parts**. Man is in his image." (James S. White, The Personality of God, 1861; Forgotten Pillar, p. 109)

> "We now see the Scriptures clearly teach, that **God is a person with a body and form**. Then Genesis 1:26, may be understood to teach the fact, that **man was made in the form of God**... This testimony cannot apply to a spirit, or immaterial part of man: that which is in the image of God has blood." (J. N. Loughborough, Review and Herald, Sep 18, 1855; Forgotten Pillar, p. 97, 106)

This understanding was not merely an undeveloped stage but a core belief derived directly from Scripture (e.g., Daniel 7:9, Exodus 33:20-23, Hebrews 1:3, John 14:9) and confirmed by Ellen White's visions.

> "I have often seen the lovely Jesus, that **He is a person**. I asked Him **if His Father was a person, and had a form like Himself**. Said Jesus, **‘I am the express image of My Father’s person!’**" (EGW, Early Writings, p. 77; Forgotten Pillar, p. 31, 45, 55, 219)

Ellen White declared this foundational truth, established after 1844, to be unchanging:

> "The past fifty years have not dimmed **one jot or principle of our faith** as we received the great and wonderful evidences that were made certain to us in 1844, after the passing of the time. ... **Not a word is changed or denied**. That which the Holy Spirit testified to as truth after the passing of the time, in our great disappointment, is the **solid foundation of truth. Pillars of truth** were revealed, and we **accepted the foundation principles** that have made us what we are—Seventh-day Adventists, keeping the commandments of God and having the faith of Jesus." (EGW, Lt 326-1905.3; Forgotten Pillar, p. 200, 231, 238)

Reducing the pioneers' stance to mere "error" overlooks the biblical basis and prophetic confirmation they believed supported this foundational pillar.

**2. Pioneer Rejection of the Trinity: Reasoned Stance, Not Ignorance**

Following from the above, the pioneers' rejection of the Trinity doctrine was not simply due to "Christian Connexion baggage" or a lack of study. Their writings, as documented in "The Forgotten Pillar," show a reasoned rejection based on their conviction that the Trinity doctrine was unbiblical and, crucially, **destroyed the Personality of God and the distinct Sonship of Christ** as they understood it from Scripture.

> "Here we might mention **the Trinity, which does away the personality of God, and of his Son Jesus Christ**..." (James White, Review & Herald, Dec 11, 1855; Forgotten Pillar, p. 54, 127)

> "The doctrine of the Trinity which was established in the church by the council of Nicea, A. D. 325. **This doctrine destroys the personality of God, and his Son Jesus Christ** our Lord..." (J. N. Andrews, Review & Herald, March 6, 1855; Forgotten Pillar, p. 54, 133, 135)

> "To believe that doctrine [Trinity], when reading the scripture we must believe that God sent himself into the world, died to reconcile the world to himself, raised himself from the dead, ascended to himself in heaven, pleads before himself... **and a thousand other such absurdities**." (J.N. Loughborough, Review & Herald, Nov 5, 1861; Forgotten Pillar, p. 132)

They saw the Trinity as introducing concepts like a "three-one God" or God being "without body or parts" (as seen in Catholic and Methodist creeds they critiqued, Forgotten Pillar p. 122), which directly contradicted their understanding of the Father's literal personality and Christ's literal Sonship.

**3. Ellen G. White's Writings: Contextual Understanding vs. Proof-Texting**

Brother Scott highlights several later Ellen White statements frequently used to support her belief in the Trinity. While these statements are authentic, "The Forgotten Pillar" argues that interpreting them contextually reveals a different picture than often presented.

- **Christ's Divinity and Eternity:** Statements like "original, unborrowed, underived" (Desire of Ages) and "God essentially... from all eternity" affirm Christ's full divinity and eternal pre-existence. However, Ellen White consistently framed this within His identity as the **"only begotten Son,"** inheriting His Father's nature and attributes, thus being "equal with God" in that sense, but not negating the Father-Son relationship or the Father's unique position as the ultimate Source ("one God").
    
    > "A complete offering has been made; for ‘God so loved the world, that he gave his **only-begotten Son**,’—not a son by creation... nor a son by adoption... but a **Son begotten in the express image of the Father’s person**, and in all the brightness of his majesty and glory, **one equal with God** in authority, dignity, and divine perfection." (EGW, ST, May 30, 1895; Forgotten Pillar, p. 130, 181)  
    > "The Lord Jesus Christ, the **only begotten Son of the Father, is truly God in infinity, but not in personality**." (EGW, Ms 116-1905.19; Forgotten Pillar, p. 130) This last quote specifically differentiates between Christ's infinite divine nature and the unique "Personality" ascribed primarily to the Father as the one God.
    
- **"Heavenly Trio" / "Three Living Persons":** The book provides detailed analysis (Chapter XVIII) showing that the prominent "heavenly trio" quote (Ms 21, 1906) was written while Ellen White was directly refuting the Trinitarian concepts presented by William Boardman in his book "Higher Christian Life." Boardman used illustrations (light, dew, rain) to represent "three living personalities" constituting "the one God" or the "Triune God." Ellen White quoted his illustrations, declared them "spiritualistic representations," "simply nothingness," "imperfect, untrue," because they inaccurately depicted the Father who "cannot be described by the things of earth" and who "is all the fulness of the Godhead **bodily**." (Forgotten Pillar, p. 171-179). She then contrasts Boardman's "three living persons of the living God" with the biblical reality of "**three living persons of the heavenly trio**"—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—who are distinct powers pledged to cooperate in salvation, not a three-in-one mysterious entity. Her use of "trio" (simply a group of three) was a direct correction of, not an endorsement of, the Trinitarian "three-in-one" concept she was instructed "not to be trusted." (Forgotten Pillar, p. 183).
    
- **Holy Spirit's Personality:** Ellen White does refer to the Holy Spirit as a "person" and possessing "personality." Brother Scott rightly points this out. However, "The Forgotten Pillar" (Chapter XV, XVII) argues she uses these terms functionally, emphasizing the Spirit's distinct agency, intelligence, and ability to interact (bear witness, search, teach), not to equate His mode of being with the Father and Son. The crucial difference lies in form. The Father and Son are personal Beings with form ("express image"). The Holy Spirit, while a distinct divine personality, is Spirit, Their representative, a "spiritual manifestation" making Their power and presence felt everywhere, distinct from Their "outward, visible manifestation."
    
    > "That **Christ** should manifest Himself to them, and yet be **invisible** to the world, was a mystery... They were thinking of the **outward, visible manifestation**. They could not take in the fact that they could have the presence of Christ with them, and yet He be unseen by the world. They did not understand the meaning of a **spiritual manifestation**." (EGW, ST, Nov 18, 1897; Forgotten Pillar, p. 156)  
    > "The Holy Spirit is the Comforter, in Christ's name. **He personifies Christ, yet is a distinct personality**." (EGW, Ms 93, 1893 / 20MR 324.2; Cited in context, Forgotten Pillar p. 128 [similar concept]) – He represents Christ's personality but is distinct.
    
    Interpreting the Spirit's "personality" as identical in nature and form to the Father and Son ignores this consistent distinction in Ellen White's writings and the pioneer understanding.
    

**4. Progressive Development or Prophesied Apostasy?**

The video's narrative leans towards viewing the historical shift as positive doctrinal refinement. "The Forgotten Pillar," however, presents a sobering alternative based on Ellen White's own warnings. She foresaw a time when foundational principles would be challenged and replaced.

> "The enemy of souls has sought to bring in the supposition that a **great reformation** was to take place among Seventh-day Adventists, and that this reformation would consist in **giving up the doctrines which stand as the pillars of our faith** and engaging in a process of reorganization... The principles of truth... would be discarded. **Our religion would be changed**. The **fundamental principles** that have sustained the work for the last fifty years **would be accounted as error**. **A new organization would be established**. Books of a new order would be written. A system of **intellectual philosophy** would be introduced..." (EGW, SpTB02 54.3 [1904]; Forgotten Pillar, p. 10-11, 62, 234, 250)

> "We cannot now **step off the foundation** that God has established. We cannot now enter into **any new organization**; for this would mean **apostasy from the truth**." (EGW, Ms 129-1905.6; Forgotten Pillar, p. 232, 240, 245)

The book argues that replacing the original Fundamental Principles concerning the Personality of God (particularly the first point) with the current Trinitarian doctrine precisely fits this prophetic description of changing the foundation and establishing a new religious framework based on philosophical interpretations rather than the plain, obvious meaning of Scripture as understood by the pioneers under divine guidance.

**Conclusion: A Call to Re-examine the Foundation**

Brother Scott's presentation helpfully brings attention to the historical discussions surrounding the Godhead in Adventism. However, by overlooking the specific pioneer definition of "Personality of God," decontextualizing key Ellen G. White statements, and framing the historical shift primarily as progression while minimizing warnings of apostasy, it presents an incomplete picture according to the research in "The Forgotten Pillar."

We believe the evidence strongly indicates that the original foundation of our faith included a clear understanding of the Father as the one God, a personal, literal Being, with Christ as His truly begotten Son, equal in nature but distinct in personality, and the Holy Spirit as Their divine, personal Representative. Ellen White consistently defended this foundation.

The "truth is in the details," and the context surrounding the development of the Godhead doctrine within Adventism is complex but crucial. We invite Brother Scott and all sincere students of Adventist history to prayerfully re-examine the evidence presented in "The Forgotten Pillar," the writings of our pioneers, and the full context of Ellen White's testimonies. Let us heed the call:

> "Our people need to understand the reasons of our faith and our past experiences... We need to urge all to put on their spiritual eyeglasses... and **discern the true pillars of the faith**... **We need to revive the old evidences of the faith** once delivered to the saints." (EGW, SW April 5, 1904; Forgotten Pillar, p. 266-267)

May we hold fast to the foundation God established, understanding His Personality as He revealed it, and avoid the "spurious theories" that seek to remove these vital truths from their setting.
