## Adam - Picture of Christ

Since in these studies we will go in chronological order through the Bible, to see where and in what way this Covenant is revealed everywhere, here is one example of type/picture which chronologically immediately presents itself. That is Adam. Adam is a representation of Christ, and some texts in Scripture that describe Adam as the first man, describe something about Christ and about the Covenant.

Adam in Hebrew means 'man' or 'humanity'. When God created man, He gave everything to Adam to manage, everything was subjected to him and man was perfect. He made him head over everything, and father of the human race. He was a representation of Christ. When Christ redeemed us, the Father gave everything to Him and made him head over everything, because he was perfect. Christ became the second Adam who was perfect and became the Father of all who will be saved, people who will become holy and immortal.

Paul understood well that Adam is a picture or type of Christ. And he himself mentioned and explained that in several places:

> [!bible] [Romans 5:14 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+5:14&version=kjv)
> <sup>**14**</sup> Nevertheless death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over them that had not sinned after the similitude of **Adam’s transgression, who is <u>the figure</u> of him that was to come**.

Here the word for 'figure' is the word 'tupos', or type. Adam was a 'type' or representation of Christ. Here is another place where Paul mentions Adam and compares him with Christ:

> [!bible] [1 Corinthians 15:22,45-49 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+15:22-49&version=kjv)
> <sup>**22**</sup> **For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive**...
> <sup>**45**</sup> **And so** it is written, **The first man Adam was made a living soul**; **the last Adam was made a quickening spirit**.
> <sup>**46**</sup> Howbeit that was not first which is spiritual, but that which is natural; and afterward that which is spiritual.
> <sup>**47**</sup> **The first man** is of the earth, earthy: **the second man** is the Lord from heaven.
> <sup>**48**</sup> As is the earthy, such are they also that are earthy: and as is the heavenly, such are they also that are heavenly.
> <sup>**49**</sup> And as we have borne the image of the earthy, we shall also bear the image of the heavenly.

Paul says 'as in Adam', and compares him with Christ and says 'so in Christ'. The first Adam was the father of humanity, but through sin became the father of sinful humanity that dies, and the second Adam through righteousness became the Father of humanity that will be holy and immortal. To the first man was given authority over earth under the condition of obedience and faithfulness, and to the second Man is given authority both in Heaven and on earth also through the condition of obedience, but also to redeem the world. When we say under condition, we see this in this text:

> [!bible] [Philippians 2:5-11 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians+2:5-11&version=kjv)
> <sup>**5**</sup> Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus:
> <sup>**6**</sup> Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God:
> <sup>**7**</sup> But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men:
> <sup>**8**</sup> And being found in fashion as a man, he **humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross**.
> <sup>**9**</sup> **Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him**, and given him a name which is above every name:
> <sup>**10**</sup> **That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow**, of things  in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth;
> <sup>**11**</sup> And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

So, under condition, and BECAUSE Christ redeemed us with His life, therefore He is set over everything as Father and Head. If we understand this well, then we know that in some places where in the Old Testament writings Adam or **man to whom authority over everything was given** is mentioned, those texts also relate to Christ and describe something about the plan of salvation, because Adam is a representation or picture of Christ. Paul understood that and in this text from 1 Corinthians 15 he mentioned this a few verses earlier:

> [!bible] [1 Corinthians 15:27 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+15:27&version=kjv)
> <sup>**27**</sup> For he (God the Father) hath put **all things under His (Christ's) feet**. ...

Notice the expressions that Paul uses here, 'put all things under his feet'. These are expressions from Psalm 110.

Now we will see this in other places and connect. Here is another example where it is seen that Paul understood that. We read part of Psalm 8. In that psalm David describes how God subjected everything to man, when he created him.

"When I consider thy heavens, the work of thy fingers, the moon and the stars, which thou hast ordained; What is man, that thou art mindful of him? and the son of man, that thou visitest him? For thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honour. Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet..." Psalm 8:3-6

Paul understood that this text does not only speak about Adam and humanity, but also about Christ, and thereby reveals the plan of salvation, that is, the Covenant. Here is the proof:

"For unto the angels hath he not put in subjection the world to come, whereof we speak. But one in a certain place testified, saying, What is man, that thou art mindful of him? or the son of man, that thou visitest him? Thou madest him a little lower than the angels; thou crownedst him with glory and honour, and didst set him over the works of thy hands: Thou hast put all things in subjection under his feet. For in that he put all in subjection under him, he left nothing that is not put under him. But now we see not yet all things put under him. But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels for the suffering of death, crowned with glory and honour; that he by the grace of God should taste death for every man." Hebrews 2:5-9

Through Adam was given a representation that the Father will subject everything to Christ later, after He redeems us and restores us according to His image. So in the example of Adam we see something of that Covenant between the Father and the Son:

- that Christ will be made a little lower than the angels for some time and that He will redeem us (meaning that He will be incarnated by the Father - 'but a body hast thou prepared me' Heb. 10:5 meaning as man)
- that He will be glorified and that the Father will give Him everything and subject everything and make Him the Father of holy, immortal humanity

Paul understood this and so he saw that Psalm 8 which speaks about man - Adam. Here we see the use of the same words as in Psalm 110 where it says:

"The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool." Psalm 110:1

And again another example where Paul says about Christ that the Father subjected everything to Him and made Him Head over everything:

"And hath put all things under his feet, and gave him to be the head over all things to the church." Ephesians 1:22

Made Father means that He is made Head over everything, that is, Ruler over all. Paul understood all that, and we saw above in 1 Corinthians 15 that he uses precisely those expressions from Psalm 110 while describing that. And those two psalms, 8 and 110, he connected, because they use the same expressions. And so texts about Adam, the first man to whom authority was given, describe the Covenant, agreement between the Father and the Son, and what They promised to Each Other in that Covenant. Christ promised that He will redeem us (because it says that He will become lower than angels for some time), restore God's image in us (He will become our Father, which means that He will give us new birth, as Adam became the father of humanity) and so become the new Father of new humanity, and God the Father promised that He will, through that work which Christ will perform, subject everything under His feet and give into His hands, making Him Head over everything. So we see through Adam as a type of Christ that there exist connected to him also prophecies which partially describe this Covenant between the Father and the Son.
