> [!abstract]
> True prosperity is not found in ambition, but in character. The Bible teaches that sustainable success is the fruit of diligence, wise communication, and godly relationships. Ultimately, all business conduct is governed by Christ's command to treat others as we wish to be treated, framing our work not as a pursuit of hasty riches, but as a faithful service that leads to blessing. This culminates in the command of Christ: to seek first the Kingdom of God, trusting that He provides for those who align their work with His will.
## Introduction

What is the true measure of a successful life or business? Can ancient wisdom from the Bible offer a real, competitive advantage in today's fast-paced world? How does recognizing God as the ultimate owner of everything fundamentally change the way we approach our daily work, our relationships, and our finances?

## The Foundation of Diligence

The scriptures begin by establishing a foundational principle: *diligent, focused work* is a path to honor and provision.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 22:29 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+22:29&version=kjv)
> <sup>**29**</sup> Seest thou a man **diligent in his business**? he shall stand before kings; he shall not stand before mean men.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 14:23 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+14:23&version=kjv)
> <sup>**23**</sup> **In all labour there is profit**: but the talk of the lips tendeth only to penury.

Conversely, a lack of diligence, characterized by sloth and indulgence, leads inevitably to want.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 13:4, 23:21 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+13:4,Proverbs+23:21&version=kjv)
> <sup>**13:4**</sup> The soul of the sluggard desireth, and hath nothing: but **the soul of the diligent shall be made fat**.
> <sup>**23:21**</sup> For the drunkard and the glutton shall come to poverty: and **drowsiness shall clothe a man with rags**.

## The Wisdom of Conduct

Beyond our actions, our words and associations are critical. Wisdom is demonstrated by restraint in speech and by avoiding the company of those who would lead us astray.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 20:19 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+20:19&version=kjv)
> <sup>**19**</sup> He that goeth about as a talebearer revealeth secrets: therefore **meddle not with him that flattereth with his lips**.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 17:27a, 20:3b - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+17:27,Proverbs+20:3&version=kjv)
> <sup>**17:27a**</sup> He that hath knowledge **spareth his words**...
> <sup>**20:3b**</sup> ... but **every fool will be meddling**.

We are warned that choosing the way of evil has guaranteed consequences, for the company we keep directly shapes our character and destiny.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 4:14, 6:28 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+4:14,Proverbs+6:28&version=kjv)
> <sup>**4:14**</sup> **Enter not into the path of the wicked**, and go not in the way of evil men.
> <sup>**6:28**</sup> **Can one go upon hot coals**, and his feet not be burned?

> [!bible] [Proverbs 13:20 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+13:20&version=kjv)
> <sup>**20**</sup> **He that walketh with wise men shall be wise**: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed.

## The Law of Christ in Relationships

Building wise relationships requires proactive friendship, which is ultimately governed by a single, profound principle from Christ Himself.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 18:24a - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+18:24&version=kjv)
> <sup>**24**</sup> A man that hath friends **must shew himself friendly**...

The whole circle of our obligation to one another is covered by that word of Christ's:

> [!bible] [Matthew 7:12a - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+7:12&version=kjv)
> <sup>**12**</sup> Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, **do ye even so to them**...

## Integrity in Business and Finance

This same integrity must govern our financial lives. True blessing comes from faithfulness, not a frantic and unprincipled pursuit of wealth. Wealth gained improperly is fleeting, while that gathered through patient labor endures.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 28:20 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+28:20&version=kjv)
> <sup>**20**</sup> **A faithful man shall abound with blessings**: but he that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 13:11 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+13:11&version=kjv)
> <sup>**11**</sup> Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished: but **he that gathereth by labour shall increase**.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 21:6 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+21:6&version=kjv)
> <sup>**6**</sup> The getting of treasures by a lying tongue is a **vanity tossed to and fro of them that seek death**.

Financial prudence also means avoiding unnecessary debt and the entanglement of being surety for others, recognizing that the "borrower is servant to the lender."

> [!bible] [Proverbs 22:7b, 11:15 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+22:7,Proverbs+11:15&version=kjv)
> <sup>**22:7b**</sup> ...the **borrower is servant to the lender**.
> <sup>**11:15**</sup> He that is surety for a stranger shall smart for it: and **he that hateth suretiship is sure**.

Finally, there is a stark warning against injustice. Exploiting the vulnerable is not only morally wrong but invites divine retribution.

> [!bible] [Proverbs 23:10-11, 22:16, 26:27 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+23:10-11;+Proverbs+22:16;+Proverbs+26:27&version=KJV)
> <sup>**23:10**</sup> Remove not the old landmark; and enter not into the fields of the fatherless:
> <sup>**23:11**</sup> For **their redeemer is mighty**; he shall plead their cause with thee. 
> <sup>**22:16**</sup> He that oppresseth the poor to increase his riches, and he that giveth to the rich, **shall surely come to want**.
> <sup>**26:27**</sup> Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein: and he that rolleth a stone, **it will return upon him**.

## The True Foundation of Success

These are principles with which are bound up the well-being of society. For all that makes confidence and co-operation possible, the world is indebted to the law of God. Even in this age of passionate competition, it is still widely acknowledged that *integrity*, *diligence*, *temperance*, *purity*, and *thrift* constitute a better capital than any amount of mere money.

That which lies at the foundation of business integrity and of *true success* is the recognition of God's ownership. The Creator of all things, He is the original proprietor. We are His stewards. All that we have is a trust from Him, to be used according to His direction.

Thus our business or calling is a part of God's great plan. As “*laborers together with God*” ([1 Corinthians  3:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+3.9&version=KJV)), our part is faithful compliance with His directions. Thus there is no place for anxious care. Our dependence will be, not on the successful outcome of our efforts, but on the promise of God. The word that fed Israel in the desert has the same power today.

> [!bible] [Matthew 6:31-33 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+6:31-33&version=KJV)
> <sup>**31**</sup> Therefore take no thought, saying, What shall we eat? or, What shall we drink? or, Wherewithal shall we be clothed?
> <sup>**32**</sup> (For after all these things do the Gentiles seek:) for your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things.
> <sup>**33**</sup> But **seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness**; and all these things shall be added unto you.

## Conclusion

The principles of Proverbs are not merely tips for a better life, but a call to reorient our entire perspective. True prosperity—in our work, relationships, and finances—flows from one source: ceasing to be anxious owners and faithfully becoming Christ-led stewards who seek His Kingdom above all else.
