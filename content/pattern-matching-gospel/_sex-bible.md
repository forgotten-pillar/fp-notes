## Introduction: The Divine Ordinance of "One Flesh"

In an age where the divine institution of marriage is besieged by confusion, a return to its biblical foundations is not merely a theological exercise, but an urgent necessity. At the heart of this foundation lies a profound concept, expressed in the very narrative of creation: the union of "one flesh." To grasp God's original intent for human sexuality and its covenantal purpose, we must first turn to its inception in the Word of God, the sole standard for all doctrine and practice.

The first divine mandate given to humanity, recorded in Genesis, establishes the primary context for the man-woman relationship.

> [!bible] [Genesis 1:27-28 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+1:27-28&version=kjv)
> <sup>**27**</sup> So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.
> <sup>**28**</sup> And God blessed them, and God said unto them, **Be fruitful, and multiply**, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

This primordial command, "Be fruitful, and multiply," was not an afterthought but the foundational purpose for which humanity, created male and female, was brought into being. The sexual union was divinely ordained from the beginning as the means by which God's people would fulfill His purpose, flourishing within the sacred institution of marriage. The nature of this institution is defined with profound simplicity and depth in the subsequent chapter.

> [!bible] [Genesis 2:24 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+2:24&version=kjv)
> <sup>**24**</sup> Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, **and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be <u>one flesh</u>**.

> [!important] Definition of marriage
> [Genesis  2:24](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+2.24&version=KJV) is the Biblical definition of marriage. It is a relation when the men is cleaved unto his wife, and they shall be **one flesh**. Jesus had confirmed it in [Matthew  19:4-6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+19.4-6&version=KJV) and [Mark  10:6-9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+10.6-9&version=KJV).

What, then, is the precise nature of this "one flesh" union? What form of unity does it represent? The apostle Paul, under divine inspiration, provides an unequivocal clarification.

> [!bible] [1 Corinthians 6:16 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+6:16-17&version=kjv)
> <sup>**16**</sup> What? know ye not that he which is **joined to an harlot is <u>one body</u>**? **for two, saith he, shall be <u>one flesh</u>**.

The apostle's direct reference to Genesis 2:24 in this context reveals the literal, physical reality of the "one flesh" union. It's the act of sexual intercourse itself that forges this bond, making two individuals "one body." This apostolic interpretation establishes a critical, non-negotiable principle: **the "one flesh" union, which serves as a defining characteristic of marriage, is an act of such profound covenantal significance that it is reserved for the marital relationship exclusively**. Engaging in this act with a harlot constitutes a violation of this sacred principle, an act of adultery.

This reveals a simple yet profound truth: **sexual intercourse is the physical seal of the marriage covenant.**
## Part 1: The "Great Mystery" – A Physical Type of a Spiritual Reality

> [!important] Pattern Matching
> In [1 Corinthians 6:16-17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+6.16-17&version=KJV) Paul is drawing a parallel between "*one flesh*" and "*one spirit*". There is a match between physical intimacy and spiritual intimacy.

The apostle does not merely define a physical act; he elevates it by drawing a divinely intended parallel between the physical union of "one flesh" and the spiritual union with the Lord.

> [!bible] [1 Corinthians 6:16-17 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+6:16-17&version=kjv)
> <sup>**16**</sup> What? know ye not that he which is **joined to an harlot** is **one body**? for two, saith he, shall be **one flesh**.
> <sup>**17**</sup> But he that is **joined unto the Lord** is **one spirit**.

Thus, the sexual union within marriage is revealed to be more than a procreative or relational act; it's a divinely ordained object lesson, a physical typology designed to teach humanity about the profound intimacy of a spiritual relationship with God. The tragedy of our fallen world is that this sacred symbol has been so degraded that its divine message is often lost. To comprehend the mystery it contains, we must first restore our understanding of sex to its exalted place in God's plan.

The full scope of this typology is unveiled in Paul's epistle to the Ephesians, where he again quotes Genesis 2:24.

> [!bible] [Ephesians 5:31-32 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+5:31-32&version=kjv)
> <sup>**31**</sup> For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and **they two shall be <u>one flesh</u>**.
> <sup>**32**</sup> **This is a great mystery**: but I speak concerning **<u>Christ and the church</u>**.

Here, the "one flesh" union is explicitly identified as a profound symbol—a "great mystery"—that speaks directly of the intimate, covenantal relationship between Christ and His church. The Gospel story itself is thus embedded within the ordinance of marriage. However uncomfortable the topic may seem, it's imperative that we proceed to explore God's high ideal for this union to unlock the "great mystery" it was designed to reveal.
## Part 2: The Indissoluble Nature of the Covenantal Bond

Our Lord Himself affirmed the profound and enduring nature of this bond. In His discourse on divorce, Christ establishes that the "one flesh" covenant possesses a permanence that transcends legal dissolution, demanding a standard of exclusivity that is absolute.

> [!bible] [Matthew 5:31-32 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+5:31-32&version=kjv)
> <sup>**31**</sup> It hath been said, Whosoever shall put away his wife, let him give her a writing of divorcement:
> <sup>**32**</sup> But I say unto you, **That whosoever shall put away his wife**, saving for the cause of fornication, **causeth her to commit adultery**: and **whosoever shall marry her that is divorced committeth adultery**.

Setting aside the explicit exception clause—"saving for the cause of fornication"—we must examine the core principle. Christ's declaration that a man who divorces his wife "causeth her to commit adultery" is predicated on the understanding that the original "one flesh" bond remains intact in God's sight. The cause of *her* subsequent adultery is not the divorce itself, but her remarriage to another man, a union which necessarily involves sexual intercourse. That act, and the marriage to the one who is divorced, is defined by Christ as adultery.

This establishes the sexual union as a covenantal token of immense significance, creating a bond so profound that it extends beyond human decrees of divorce. It reveals God's ultimate design, unmarred by sin: a singular, exclusive, lifelong "one flesh" union for every man and woman with their chosen spouse. To engage in this act with anyone other than that one covenant partner is plainly defined as adultery.
