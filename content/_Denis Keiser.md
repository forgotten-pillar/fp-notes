## Re-examining the Narrative: The Adventist Journey and the Foundational Truth of God's Personality

We appreciate Dr. Denis Kaiser's recent presentation, "From Anti-trinitarianism to Trinitarianism: The Adventist Story," and his willingness to engage with the history of Seventh-day Adventist beliefs concerning the Godhead. Such discussions are vital as we seek to understand our heritage and solidify our faith in these last days. Dr. Kaiser helpfully traces a historical trajectory, noting the shift from the largely non-Trinitarian views of our pioneers to the Church's current Trinitarian stance.

However, viewing this history through the lens presented in The Forgotten Pillar, we believe a crucial element is often overlooked, leading to a narrative that, while containing factual points, may misinterpret the core issues and the nature of the changes that occurred. Our aim here is not confrontation, but constructive dialogue, encouraging a deeper look at the foundations laid in the beginning of our movement.

### Did the Pioneers Neglect the Doctrine of God?

A key point suggested in the presentation is that early Adventists, while discovering doctrines like the Sanctuary and the Sabbath, initially "did not study the subject of the doctrine of God" (approx. 00:19:52-00:20:02). From the perspective detailed in The Forgotten Pillar, this statement requires significant qualification. While they may not have used later Trinitarian frameworks, the pioneers, under divine guidance, did establish a clear, foundational understanding of God, specifically focusing on His **personality**.

This understanding, solidified after the 1844 disappointment and forming the bedrock of their faith, was publicly and consistently articulated in the **Fundamental Principles**, first published in 1872 and reprinted for over four decades. The very first point declared:

> "That there is **one God, a personal, spiritual being, the creator of all things**, omnipotent, omniscient, and eternal; infinite in wisdom, holiness, justice, goodness, truth, and mercy; unchangeable, and **everywhere present by his representative, the Holy Spirit**. Ps. 139:7." (The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. II, quoting FP 1889)

This wasn't a neglected topic; it was their core doctrine of God. It defined the "one God" as the Father—a literal, personal, spiritual Being residing in heaven but omnipresent via His Spirit—and Christ as His literal, distinct, begotten Son (The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. III, XI, XII). This doctrine of the **personality of God**—His quality or state of being a person, including His tangible form—was considered a **pillar** and part of the **foundation** of their faith (The Forgotten Pillar, Intro, Ch. I).

### Progressive Development or Departure from the Foundation?

Dr. Kaiser presents the historical shift towards Trinitarianism as a positive, progressive development, a growth in understanding led by God. The Forgotten Pillar invites us to consider a different perspective, one rooted in the warnings of Ellen G. White herself. She cautioned against moving the "old landmarks" and establishing a "new organization," identifying such moves as "apostasy from the truth":

> "We cannot now step off the foundation that God has established. We cannot now enter into any new organization; for this would mean apostasy from the truth." (EGW, Ms129-1905.6, quoted in The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. XXVI)

The original Fundamental Principles were that foundation. If, as Dr. Kaiser notes, the pioneers grew in understanding, did this growth lead them to affirm or abandon their foundational understanding of God's personality? The evidence explored in The Forgotten Pillar suggests the latter. The pioneers explicitly rejected the Trinity precisely because it conflicted with their understanding of the personality of the Father as the "one God" and Christ as His literal Son (e.g., James White, J.N. Andrews, J.B. Frisbie, quoted in The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. V, XIV, XIII). They saw the Trinitarian concepts of "three-in-one" as contradictory to Scripture and destructive of the distinct personalities of the Father and Son.

To frame the adoption of the Trinity simply as "progress" without acknowledging its direct conflict with the explicitly stated, foundational "personality of God" doctrine risks overlooking the prophetic warnings about removing the pillars of our faith.

### Reinterpreting Ellen White's Stance

The presentation, like much modern scholarship, utilizes later statements from Ellen White regarding the Holy Spirit's personality and Christ's divinity and eternity as evidence of her eventual acceptance of the Trinity. The Forgotten Pillar argues that these statements, when read through the lens of the original foundation—particularly the doctrine of the presence and personality of God—do not necessitate a Trinitarian conclusion and, in fact, reinforce her consistency.

1. **The Holy Spirit's Personality:** Ellen White indeed affirmed the Spirit's personality ("He must also be a divine person," Ms 20, 1906, quoted in The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. XV). However, as detailed in Chapter XV and XIX, she and the pioneers distinguished between the type of personality. The Father and Son possess personality associated with a tangible form and location. The Spirit's personality relates to intelligence, will, and action, functioning as the omnipresent representative of the Father and Son—a "spiritual manifestation" distinct from the "outward, visible manifestation" of Christ (ST Nov 18, 1897, quoted in The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. XV). Equating these distinct concepts of personality, as Trinitarianism does, was the very point of contention during the Kellogg crisis.
    
2. **Christ's Divinity and Eternity:** Ellen White powerfully affirmed Christ's full divinity and eternal pre-existence ("Christ was God essentially... He was with God from all eternity," RH April 5, 1906, quoted in The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. XXVI). This aligns perfectly with the pioneer understanding that Christ, as the begotten Son (not created), inherited the Father's divine nature fully, making Him "one equal with God in authority, dignity, and divine perfection" (ST May 30, 1895, quoted in The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. XIV). His equality stemmed from His inheritance as the Son, not from being part of an amalgamated "three-one God."
    
3. **The "Heavenly Trio" and Lt253-1903:** Perhaps the most crucial evidence involves the context of statements often cited for Trinitarianism.
    
    - The "Heavenly Trio" quotation (Ms21-1906), as analyzed in Chapter XVIII of The Forgotten Pillar, appears within a manuscript where EGW directly refutes William Boardman's Trinitarian ("tri-personality of the one God") sentiments, calling his illustrative representations "spiritualistic" and "nothingness." She contrasts Boardman's "Triune God" with her understanding of God: "**The Father** is all the fulness of the Godhead bodily and is invisible to mortal sight." Her reference to the "three living persons of the heavenly trio" affirms three divine entities (Father, Son, Spirit-representative) cooperating for salvation, not three persons constituting one God, which she had just labeled untrustworthy.
        
    - In Lt253-1903 (The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. V), written after being informed of Dr. Kellogg's recent acceptance of the Trinity, Ellen White directly rebukes his plan to "patch up" Living Temple with Trinitarian ideas. She calls these "**patchwork theories**" that "**cannot be accepted by those who are loyal to the faith and to the principles that have withstood all the opposition of satanic influences**." She contrasts these new theories with the established "leading points of our faith," reaffirming her visions about the distinct, personal Father and Son. This letter stands as powerful testimony against her acceptance of the Trinity.
        

When viewed through the established foundation, Ellen White's writings maintain a remarkable consistency, upholding the personality of the Father and the Son as distinct Beings while affirming the Spirit's divine personality as Their representative. The narrative of her "conversion" to Trinitarianism arises primarily when the original context and the foundational pillar of God's personality are obscured.

### The Bottom of the Issue: A Foundational Choice

As The Forgotten Pillar argues (Ch. XXII), the core difference lies in hermeneutics—how we interpret Scripture regarding God's person. Do we, like the pioneers, understand terms like "Father," "Son," and "person" in their plain, obvious meaning, accepting God's revelation that He has a form and Christ is His literal Son? Or do we adopt a "theological sense," as Dr. Kaiser mentions modern Adventism does (ref. Sabbath School lesson quoted in The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. XXIII), where these terms become metaphorical representations of relationships within a mysterious, formless Triune God?

Ellen White was clear: "The language of the Bible should be explained according to its obvious meaning, unless a symbol or figure is employed" (GC 598). She warned against speculating on God's nature and personality beyond what "the language of the Word" reveals (Lt232-1903, quoted in The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. XXIX).

### Conclusion: A Call to Remember

Dr. Kaiser rightly highlights that the pioneers were led by God and grew in understanding. The Forgotten Pillar affirms this but emphasizes that this leading established a specific foundation, including the doctrine of God's personality, which was never to be removed. The subsequent shift toward Trinitarianism, rather than being the intended trajectory of that leading, appears as a departure from the very principles God established through those pioneers and confirmed through the Spirit of Prophecy.

We are called to "hold fast the first principles of our denominated faith" and to rehearse the history of how God led us (The Forgotten Pillar, Ch. XXIII, XXVIII, quoting EGW). This includes a careful, honest study of the Fundamental Principles and the foundational pillar concerning the personality of God. Only by understanding the truths laid at the beginning can we accurately discern the nature of the changes that followed and respond faithfully to God's call in these last days.

We invite all, including scholars like Dr. Kaiser, to re-engage with these foundational truths, not as relics of an immature past, but as the God-given platform upon which our faith was built and upon which, according to prophecy, it must be re-established. Let us focus on the clear, authoritative Word and the foundational principles it revealed, allowing them to guide our understanding of God and our history.

## Re-Examining Our Historical Compass: A Response to "From Anti-trinitarianism to Trinitarianism"

We appreciate the diligent historical work presented by Dr. Denis Kaiser in his presentation, "From Anti-trinitarianism to Trinitarianism: The Adventist Story." Tracing the shifts in theological understanding within our church is valuable. However, from the perspective detailed in "The Forgotten Pillar" [further refferred as TFP], we believe a crucial element is often overlooked in narratives focusing solely on a perceived "progressive development" towards Trinitarianism. This oversight risks obscuring the original foundation of our faith and the specific reasons our pioneers, guided by the Spirit of Prophecy, stood apart on the doctrine of God.

Our aim here is not confrontation, but constructive dialogue – a call to re-examine our history by placing the original Fundamental Principles and the pillar doctrine of the Personality of God back at the center of the discussion.

### The Foundation Stone: Did Pioneers Neglect the Godhead?

A common assertion, reflected in Dr. Kaiser's presentation (https://youtu.be/sxLA1gGQCwo?t=1160), is that the early Sabbatarian Adventists, while diligently studying doctrines like the Sabbath and the Sanctuary, *"did not study the subject of the doctrine of God. That wasn't a question that they dealt with at that time."*

From the perspective of "The Forgotten Pillar," this assertion requires careful scrutiny. While it's true their *primary* focus post-1844 was understanding the disappointment and the unfolding truths of the Sanctuary and the Three Angels' Messages, this very work *necessitated* a clear understanding of the God they were called to worship (Rev 14:7). The **Fundamental Principles**, first published officially in 1872 but representing the faith hammered out since 1844, stand as direct evidence *against* the claim that the Godhead was unstudied.

As Ellen White recalled:

> "Many of our people do not realize **how firmly the foundation of our faith has been laid**. My husband, Elder Joseph Bates, Father Pierce, Elder Edson, and others who were keen, noble, and true, were among those who, after the passing of the time in 1844, searched for the truth as for hidden treasure... Thus light was given that helped us to understand the scriptures in regard to Christ, His mission, and His priesthood. **A line of truth extending from that time to the time when we shall enter the city of God, was made plain to me**, and I gave to others the instruction that the Lord had given me." (EGW, SpTB02 56.4; 1904 - TFP, Ch. I, p. 16-17)

The first point of the 1872/1889 Fundamental Principles *is* a definitive statement on the Godhead:

> "That there is **one God, a personal, spiritual being, the creator of all things**, omnipotent, omniscient, and eternal; infinite in wisdom, holiness, justice, goodness, truth, and mercy; unchangeable, and **everywhere present by his representative, the Holy Spirit**. Ps. 139:7." (FP 1889, I - TFP, Appendix, p. 360)

This wasn't a peripheral point; it was Point I. It defined the God of the first angel's message – the Creator Father. This *was* the result of their study on the Godhead, guided by the Spirit.

### The Forgotten Pillar: The Personality of God

Central to the pioneers' understanding, and a core thesis of "The Forgotten Pillar," was the doctrine of the **Personality of God**. As explored in chapters X, XI, and XIII of TFP, pioneers like J. N. Loughborough, James White, and J. B. Frisbie explicitly understood this to mean that God the Father possesses a literal, tangible form and body, residing in a specific location (heaven), from which His Son, Jesus Christ, also a distinct personal being with form, was begotten. His omnipresence was understood through the agency of His Holy Spirit, His "representative."

This understanding was not vague; it was a specific, defined doctrine that formed a crucial "pillar" of their faith. James White wrote:

> "MAN was made in the image of God... Those who deny the **personality of God**, say that ‘image’ here does not mean **physical form**, but moral image... As proof that **God is a person**, read his own words to Moses... Here God tells Moses that he shall see **his form**. To say that God made it appear to Moses that he saw his form, **when he has no form, is charging God with adding to falsehood a sort of juggling deception** upon his servant Moses." (James S. White, PERGO 1.1; 1861 - TFP, Ch. XI, p. 127)

Their rejection of the Trinity was not born of ignorance, but of conviction based on this understanding. They saw the Trinity doctrine as fundamentally incompatible with the biblical truth of the distinct, literal personalities of the Father and the Son. James White famously called the Trinity one of the "popular fables of the age," stating it *"does away the personality of God, and of his Son Jesus Christ."* (Review & Herald, Dec 11, 1855 - TFP, Ch. XIV, p. 158). J.N. Andrews stated the Nicene doctrine *"destroys the personality of God, and his Son Jesus Christ our Lord..."* (Review & Herald, March 6, 1855 - TFP, Ch. V, p. 67).

Narratives focusing on a linear progression towards Trinitarianism often fail to give sufficient weight to *this* foundational pillar and the specific reasons it led pioneers to reject the Trinity doctrine offered by other denominations.

### Re-Interpreting the Evidence: Kellogg, Ellen White, and the Foundation

The Kellogg crisis is pivotal. As detailed in TFP (Ch. I, IV, V, XV), the core issue with Kellogg's "Living Temple" was its subtle undermining of the **Personality of God** and His presence, stepping away from the first Fundamental Principle. Ellen White identified this precisely:

> "[Living Temple] introduces that which is naught but **speculation in regard to the personality of God and where His presence is**." (EGW, SpTB02 51.3; 1904 - TFP, Ch. I, p. 10)

Kellogg's later embrace of Trinitarian concepts (as documented by A.G. Daniells, TFP Ch. IV, p. 46) appears, from this perspective, as a *justification* for his initial deviation from the FPs, rather than the primary error itself.

Ellen White's response was not to refine the Trinity doctrine, separating it from Kellogg's pantheistic leanings. Instead, she consistently pointed back to the *original foundation* and warned against changing its pillars:

> "Those who seek to remove the old landmarks are not holding fast... Those who try to bring in theories that would remove **the pillars of our faith concerning the sanctuary, or concerning the personality of God or of Christ**, are working as blind men." (EGW, Ms62-1905.14; 1905 - TFP, Ch. I, p. 4)
>
> *"I must tell you that your ideas in regard to some things have been decidedly wrong... The book Living Temple is **not to be patched up**... When you wrote that book, you were not under the inspiration of God... Just such theories as you have presented in Living Temple were presented then [early days]... But I have ever had the same testimony to bear which I now bear **regarding the personality of God**."* (EGW, Lt253-1903; TFP, Ch. V, p. 52, 55)

Even the often-cited "Heavenly Trio" quotation (Ms21-1906), when examined in its full context (TFP, Ch. XVIII), reveals Ellen White *refuting* William Boardman's Trinitarian illustrations (which she called "spiritualistic representations" and "nothingness"), and specifically defending the **Father's** distinct personality and visibility (to non-mortals), in direct contrast to Boardman's "invisible" Father within a "Triune God." She upheld the distinctness of the Father and Son as personal beings, whose unity was one of purpose, mind, and character, *"but not in person."* (EGW, MH 421-422; 1905 - TFP, Ch. XIV, p. 160).

The narrative of Ellen White leading the church *into* Trinitarianism struggles against her explicit, repeated warnings *against* removing the foundational pillars, particularly the one defining the **Personality of God** as understood by the pioneers.

### Progressive Truth or Foundational Replacement?

The historical data shows a shift, yes. But was it merely "progressive understanding," or was it, as Ellen White warned, a "reorganization" involving discarding *"the principles of truth that God in His wisdom has given to the remnant church"* and accounting *"the fundamental principles that have sustained the work for the last fifty years... as error"*? (EGW, SpTB02 54.3; 1904 - TFP, Ch. XXIV, p. 294).

"The Forgotten Pillar" argues that adopting the Trinity doctrine necessitated stepping off the original foundation regarding the Personality of God. The two views – one God being the Father with a distinct Son and Spirit representative vs. one God existing as three co-equal, co-eternal persons – are based on fundamentally different interpretive principles (literal vs. metaphorical/theological for terms like Father, Son, Personhood of God). They cannot be harmonized; one replaces the other. Ellen White warned:

> *"We cannot now step off the foundation that God has established. We cannot now enter into any new organization; for this would mean apostasy from the truth."* (EGW, Ms129-1905.6; 1905 - TFP, Ch. XXVI, p. 307)

### A Call to Remember Our Landmarks

Dr. Kaiser rightly highlights the development of thought within Adventism (https://youtu.be/sxLA1gGQCwo?t=2748). We concur that our pioneers grew in understanding. However, understanding this growth requires acknowledging the starting point – the foundational Fundamental Principles established by God through intense Bible study and confirmed by the Spirit of Prophecy. The doctrine of the Personality of God was not an incidental viewpoint; it was a load-bearing pillar, essential to their understanding of the God of the Bible and the reason they could not accept the Trinity.

Ignoring or downplaying this pillar leads to a distorted view of our history and identity. It makes Ellen White's strenuous defenses of the "foundation" and "landmarks" seem disconnected or even contradictory.

We believe a return to studying the Fundamental Principles, the specific arguments of our pioneers like White, Loughborough, Andrews, and Frisbie regarding the Personality of God (as detailed in TFP), and Ellen White's writings *in their original context*, is crucial. This isn't about fostering division, but about achieving clarity, understanding *why* our pioneers believed as they did, and reclaiming the unique identity God forged for this movement upon *"the platform of eternal truth."* (EGW, SpTB02 51.2; 1904 - TFP, Ch. I, p. 9).

Let us heed the counsel:

> *"The Lord has declared that the history of the past shall be rehearsed as we enter upon the closing work. Every truth that He has given for these last days is to be proclaimed to the world. Every pillar that He has established is to be strengthened."* (EGW, Ms129-1905.7; 1905 - TFP, Ch. XXVIII, p. 334)

May we rediscover the profound truths embedded in our original foundation, including the forgotten pillar of the Personality of God, and understand anew the path where the Lord has led us.

## A Constructive Dialogue: The Personality of God, Our Pioneers, and Our Journey

**By Denis Kaiser, Ph.D.**

Greetings in the precious name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

It is with genuine appreciation that I engage with the work presented in "The Forgotten Pillar" (hereafter TFP). The authors, Michael Presečan and Marjan Gerguri, have undertaken a commendable effort to bring to light significant aspects of our early Seventh-day Adventist history, particularly focusing on the foundational beliefs surrounding the personality of God and the historical context of the Kellogg crisis. As someone whose own journey into church history was sparked by questions surrounding this very topic ([DKP, 0:05:07](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D307s)), I resonate deeply with the desire to understand God's leading in our past and its implications for our present identity.

TFP rightly emphasizes several key historical points:

1. **The Foundational Importance of God's Personality:** TFP correctly identifies that the concept of God's personality was indeed a crucial element, a "pillar" (TFP, p. 4, 9, 14), in the framework of early Adventist belief, particularly as articulated against the backdrop of spiritualizing interpretations prevalent after 1844 (TFP, p. 36-41).
    
2. **Link to Fundamental Principles (1872/1889):** The book accurately connects this understanding to the early Fundamental Principles, especially the first point, which declared belief in "one God, a personal, spiritual being, the creator of all things... everywhere present by his representative, the Holy Spirit" (TFP, p. 27, 31, 93, Appendix p. 360).
    
3. **Pioneer Anti-Trinitarianism:** TFP provides ample evidence, quoting figures like James White, J.N. Loughborough, J.B. Frisbie, and J.N. Andrews, demonstrating that many, if not most, early Adventist pioneers held anti-Trinitarian views (TFP, p. 67, 77, 123-178). They often saw the doctrine of the Trinity as unbiblical, potentially obscuring the distinct personalities of the Father and the Son, or even rooted in pagan concepts (TFP, p. 168, 173-176).
    
4. **The Kellogg Crisis:** TFP highlights the Kellogg crisis ("Living Temple") and positions the deviation regarding the "personality of God and where His presence is" (TFP, p. 21, 30-31, 45, 103) as central to that controversy, arguing against interpretations that focus solely on pantheism.
    

These points are historically valuable and underscore the need for us, as Seventh-day Adventists, to honestly engage with our theological heritage. TFP's call to remember "the way the Lord has led us, and His teaching in our past history" (TFP, p. 3, quoting LS 196.2) is one I wholeheartedly endorse.

However, where my perspective, informed by extensive research into the primary documents (letters, manuscripts, periodicals – many available online at [AdventistArchives.org](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.adventistarchives.org%2F) and [EGWWritings.org](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fegwwritings.org%2F), [DKP, 0:11:26](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D686s)), diverges is in the interpretation of how God led us. TFP presents the early anti-Trinitarian understanding, particularly concerning the Father's literal form and location, as the definitive and unchanging "foundation" (TFP, p. 9, 25, 75-76), suggesting that the later adoption of Trinitarian beliefs constitutes an "apostasy" (TFP, p. 283, 290) from that original truth.

My research suggests a different narrative: one of **progressive understanding** under the guidance of the Holy Spirit. The early pioneers, while sincere and God-led, did not receive the entire picture at once ([DKP, 1:28:06](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D5286s)). Their initial focus after 1844 was, understandably, on doctrines like the Sanctuary, the Sabbath, and the Three Angels' Messages, which explained the Disappointment and established our unique prophetic identity. The doctrine of God itself wasn't their primary subject of deep, systematic study in those earliest years ([DKP, 0:19:49](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D1189s)).

The anti-Trinitarian views they brought into the movement often stemmed from their previous affiliations (like the Christian Connexion for White and Bates) which reacted against creedalism and philosophical constructs they felt obscured biblical simplicity ([DKP, 0:19:26](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D1166s)). Their understanding, often described as semi-Arian (believing Christ was begotten or emanated from the Father in time, thus having a beginning, unlike the Nicene/Catholic view of eternal generation in timelessness), reflected this background ([DKP, 0:14:21](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D861s) - [0:16:51](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D1011s)).

**The Shift and Development:**

The historical record shows a gradual development, particularly accelerating after the 1888 Minneapolis General Conference session, which brought a renewed focus on Christ and His righteousness. This led to deeper study concerning Christ's nature and divinity.

- **Christ's Divinity:** Pioneers like James White himself began emphasizing Christ's equality with the Father, moving away from views that might imply inferiority (TFP, p. 161, quoting James White, 1877; [DKP, 0:36:40](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D2200s)). Ellen White strongly affirmed Christ's eternal pre-existence and full equality ([DKP, 0:30:10](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D1810s), [1:11:06](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D4266s)). E.J. Waggoner argued for Christ being "of the substance of the Father, so that in his very nature he is God," while still initially holding to the idea of the Father being "before the Son" ([DKP, 0:48:30](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D2910s)). Later, figures like Uriah Smith revised earlier Arian-leaning statements to clarify Christ was the beginner of creation, not the first created ([DKP, 0:56:47](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3407s)).
    
- **The Holy Spirit's Personality:** While early views often depicted the Spirit as an influence or power, the 1890s saw a marked shift. Prompted perhaps by Ellen White's call to recognize the Spirit's work ([DKP, 1:00:02](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3602s)), individuals like G.C. Tenney ([DKP, 0:53:08](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3188s)), R.A. Underwood ([DKP, 1:01:31](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3691s)), and S.N. Haskell ([DKP, 1:10:02](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D4202s)) began affirming the Spirit's distinct personality, often quoting Ellen White's statements from The Desire of Ages (1898) about the "third person of the Godhead" ([DKP, 0:55:16](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3316s)). This wasn't isolated; it became a widespread discussion within the church during her lifetime, reflected in numerous articles quoting her on the topic ([DKP, 1:00:23](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3623s)).
    

**Harmonizing the Fundamental Principles (1889) with Trinitarianism:**

How does this developmental understanding help us reconcile the first point of the 1889 Fundamental Principles with our current Trinitarian belief? Let's re-examine it:

> "I. That there is **one God**, a **personal, spiritual being**, the creator of all things, omnipotent, omniscient, and eternal; infinite in wisdom, holiness, justice, goodness, truth, and mercy; unchangeable, and **everywhere present by his representative, the Holy Spirit**. Psalm 139:7." (TFP, Appendix p. 360)

- **"One God":** This affirms foundational monotheism, which Trinitarianism upholds. The challenge was understanding how God is one while Scripture also speaks distinctly of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
    
- **"Personal, spiritual being":** Early Adventists rightly affirmed God's personality against impersonal, spiritualizing views. Their emphasis on the Father having a form (TFP, p. 113, 125-127, 132) was crucial in establishing God's concrete reality and the literal nature of heaven and the sanctuary, countering those who allegorized them away ([DKP, 0:25:27](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D1527s)). However, this emphasis on physical form as the defining aspect of personality could be seen as an early, incomplete understanding. Trinitarianism affirms God is personal—the Father is a person, the Son is a person, the Spirit is a person ([DKP, 1:08:30](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D4110s)). Christ, as the "express image," reveals the personal reality and character of the Father (TFP, p. 130, 194), but this doesn't necessitate reducing the divine persons to purely physical limitations as we understand them. God is Spirit (John 4:24), transcending physical constraints while still being intensely personal.
    
- **"Everywhere present by his representative, the Holy Spirit":** This was the pioneers' way of reconciling God's personal location in heaven (at His throne, TFP p. 150) with His omnipresence. As the understanding of the Holy Spirit developed from an "influence" or "representative" of God to the understanding of the Spirit as God, the third person of the Godhead ([DKP, 1:03:01](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3781s), [1:06:18](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3978s)), this statement found its fuller meaning. God is omnipresent through the agency of the Holy Spirit, not because the Spirit is merely an impersonal force sent by an absent Father, but because the Spirit is the divine, personal presence of God active throughout the universe.
    

Therefore, the 1889 statement, viewed through this developmental lens, represents a snapshot in time—truthful for its context but not the final, exhaustive word on the Godhead revealed through ongoing Bible study guided by the Spirit. The later Trinitarian understanding built upon the foundational truths of God's personality and active presence, providing a fuller explanation consistent with the entirety of Scripture, including the growing emphasis on Christ's eternal divinity and the Spirit's distinct personality.

**Ellen White's Role and Conclusion:**

Ellen White's role was consistent with her prophetic ministry: confirming truth discovered through Bible study, correcting error, and urging deeper understanding ([DKP, 0:51:38](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3098s)). Her writings from the late 1890s onwards clearly support the developing understanding of the eternal equality of Father and Son and the personality of the Holy Spirit as the third person of the Godhead ([DKP, 0:55:16](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D3316s), [1:11:06](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D4266s)). To interpret her warnings against "spiritualistic theories" or "patchwork theories" (TFP, p. 64, 71, 74) solely as condemnations of Trinitarianism, while ignoring her own later affirmations and the broader context of the church's study, risks misrepresenting her guidance. Her cautions were against any theory, including flawed Trinitarian or anti-Trinitarian speculations (like Kellogg's pantheism or extreme spiritualizing), that undermined the foundational truths established through Scripture and confirmed by the Spirit.

"The Forgotten Pillar" serves as a vital reminder of where we came from and the importance of the pioneers' quest for truth. However, understanding God's leading involves embracing the entire journey, including the growth and refinement of understanding that occurred during the pioneers' lifetimes, particularly under the ministry of Ellen White. Our current understanding of the Trinity is not an "apostasy" from the foundation, but rather, arguably, the structure built upon that foundation through continued, Spirit-led study of God's Word, revealing the fullness of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit—our one, true, personal, relational God.

May we continue to study, with open hearts and minds, following the example of our pioneers, ever seeking a deeper understanding of the magnificent God we serve ([DKP, 1:28:26](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DsxLA1gGQCwo%26t%3D5306s)).

## Continuing the Dialogue: A Response to Dr. Kaiser on God's Leading and Our Foundation

**From The Forgotten Pillar Project**

We sincerely thank Dr. Denis Kaiser for engaging with the crucial history surrounding the Seventh-day Adventist understanding of God. His presentation, "From Anti-Trinitarianism to Trinitarianism: The Adventist Story," demonstrates a shared desire to understand our past. We appreciate his acknowledgment of the historical reality that our pioneers were largely non-Trinitarian and that the personality of God was a significant theme in their theology, as extensively documented in "The Forgotten Pillar" (TFP).

Dr. Kaiser rightly notes the historical materials available ([DKP, 0:11:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=686s)) and encourages personal investigation – a principle we strongly uphold. However, while we agree on many historical *facts*, our *interpretation* of God's leading through that history differs significantly, particularly concerning the concept Dr. Kaiser terms "progressive understanding."

Let us address the key points raised in Dr. Kaiser's presentation from the perspective articulated in TFP:

**1. On "Progressive Understanding" vs. an Established Foundation:**

Dr. Kaiser suggests that the pioneers' initial non-Trinitarian views, particularly regarding the personality of God, were incomplete and that the church *progressively* grew into a Trinitarian understanding ([DKP, 1:28:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=5286s), [1:29:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=5341s)).

TFP argues, based on the testimony of the Spirit of Prophecy, that the core truths constituting our foundation *were* established early on and are immutable. Ellen White stated, *"The past fifty years have not dimmed one jot or principle of our faith as we received the great and wonderful evidences that were made certain to us in 1844, after the passing of the time. ... Not a word is changed or denied. That which the Holy Spirit testified to as truth after the passing of the time, in our great disappointment, is the solid foundation of truth. Pillars of truth were revealed, and we accepted the foundation principles that have made us what we are—Seventh-day Adventists..."* (TFP, p. 250-251, quoting Lt326-1905.3). She further warned, *"We cannot now step off the foundation that God has established. We cannot now enter into any new organization; for this would mean apostasy from the truth."* (TFP, p. 290, quoting Ms129-1905.6).

The personality of God, understood as the Father being a literal, personal, spiritual being with form, and Christ His literal, begotten Son, was explicitly identified by Ellen White as a "pillar" alongside the Sanctuary doctrine (TFP, p. 315, quoting Ms62-1905.14). This wasn't an incomplete understanding awaiting progression *into* Trinitarianism; it *was* the revealed truth, the established landmark. Later changes, therefore, represent not a Spirit-led progression *on this point*, but a "reorganization" based on "intellectual philosophy" that constitutes "apostasy from the truth" (TFP, p. 294, 304, quoting Lt242-1903).

**2. On Christ's Divinity and Sonship:**

Dr. Kaiser correctly notes the pioneers, including James White and later Ellen White, strongly affirmed Christ's divinity and equality with the Father ([DKP, 0:36:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=2200s), [0:55:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=3316s)). TFP fully concurs. However, the *nature* of that equality is crucial. The pioneers and Ellen White understood Christ's divinity through the lens of literal Sonship. He is divine *because* He is the *"Son begotten in the express image of the Father’s person... one equal with God in authority, dignity, and divine perfection."* (TFP, p. 172, quoting ST May 30, 1895). His equality stems from inheritance, being of the same divine substance as His Father *because* He is His Son.

This contrasts sharply with the Trinitarian model of three co-equal, co-eternal persons who are *not* in a literal Father-Son relationship but share an undefined "Godhead" substance. The pioneers rejected this Trinitarian construct precisely because it "does away the personality of God, and of his Son Jesus Christ" by denying their distinct, literal, tangible personages and the reality of their relationship (TFP, p. 158, quoting James White, 1846 & 1855). The shift towards emphasizing Christ's *eternal* pre-existence (without beginning) in Ellen White's later writings ([DKP, 1:11:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=4266s)) does not negate His Sonship but deepens the mystery of His divine origin *from* the Father, an origin that TFP maintains is essential to understanding the Atonement and God's character.

**3. On the Holy Spirit's Personality:**

Dr. Kaiser highlights the shift in the 1890s towards recognizing the Holy Spirit's personality, citing Ellen White's 1898 statement about the "third person of the Godhead" ([DKP, 0:55:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=3316s)) and subsequent affirmations by others ([DKP, 1:01:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=3691s), [1:06:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=3978s)). TFP acknowledges Ellen White affirmed the Spirit's personality: *"The Holy Spirit has a personality, else He could not bear witness to our spirits..."* (TFP, p. 193, quoting Ms 20, 1906).

However, the *nature* of that personality is distinct from that of the Father and Son. Ellen White defined the Father's personality by His *form* like Christ's ([DKP, 0:27:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=1641s); TFP, p. 194, quoting EW 77.1). The Holy Spirit, by contrast, is *Spirit*. While possessing personal attributes (intelligence, will, ability to bear witness, teach, grieve), the Spirit does not possess a physical body or form like the Father and Son. He is the *representative* of the Father and Son, the very presence and power *of* Christ made manifest spiritually, distinct from Christ's outward, visible manifestation (TFP, p. 195, quoting ST Nov 18, 1897).

The Trinitarian view, which Dr. Kaiser's presentation reflects, tends to equalize the *mode* of personality among the Three, blurring the distinction clearly maintained by the pioneers and Ellen White. TFP argues that applying the understanding of the Spirit's personality (without bodily form) *exclusively* to the Father leads to the spiritualizing errors Ellen White warned against (TFP, p. 192, 195). The "third person" terminology, while used by Ellen White, must be understood within her consistent framework distinguishing the *nature* of the Spirit's personality from that of the Father and Son.

**4. On Harmonizing the 1889 Statement:**

Dr. Kaiser suggests the 1889 statement can be seen as a step towards Trinitarianism. TFP maintains its meaning is clear within the non-Trinitarian context of its time. *"One God"* refers explicitly to the Father (as subsequent points clarify Christ is the *Son* of the Eternal Father). He is a *"personal, spiritual being"* – personal, having form and substance, yet spiritual in nature (like angels are "spiritual beings" with bodies, TFP p. 154). His omnipresence is not inherent to His personal being (which is localized in heaven, TFP p. 150) but is accomplished *"by his representative, the Holy Spirit."*

To harmonize this with Trinitarianism requires altering the "obvious meaning" (TFP, p. 270, quoting GC 598.3) of these terms. It requires redefining "one God" to mean a tri-personal substance, redefining "personal, spiritual being" to potentially exclude tangible form for the Father, and redefining the Spirit from God's *representative* for omnipresence to being God *Himself* omnipresent alongside the Father and Son. TFP holds that the original, plain meaning of the 1889 statement is unequivocally non-Trinitarian and reflects the established foundation.

**5. On Ellen White's Role and the "Heavenly Trio":**

The assertion that Ellen White led the church *into* Trinitarianism is, from TFP's perspective, a fundamental misreading of her ministry. While she interacted with developing ideas, her consistent testimony defended the original pillars *against* encroaching errors that resembled Trinitarian concepts. Her rebuke of Kellogg involved defending the *personality of God* (the Father) against his speculative, spiritualizing theories which, as TFP shows, were justified by Kellogg through a Trinitarian lens (TFP, p. 174-175, 185-187, 202).

Her use of terms like "third person" or "heavenly trio" (TFP, p. 224, 229) must be understood within her broader theological framework, *not* imposed upon by later Trinitarian assumptions. As TFP demonstrates with the "Heavenly Trio" quotation (Ms21-1906), the context shows Ellen White was refuting William Boardman's *Trinitarian* illustrations (three living persons *of one living God*), correcting his view by affirming *the Father* (not a Triune God) is the one who "is all the fulness of the Godhead bodily and is invisible to mortal sight," while the Son is the fullness manifested, and the Spirit is the fullness in action (TFP, p. 222-229). She used "trio" to refer to the three distinct divine agents, but explicitly contrasted Boardman's "tri-personality of the one God" with the truth about *the Father*. She warned against the Trinitarian sentiments Boardman presented (TFP, p. 214, 220).

**Conclusion: The Unchanging Foundation**

Dr. Kaiser speaks of pioneers being open to discover "new things" ([DKP, 1:28:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxLA1gGQCwo&t=5329s)). We agree, but differentiate between discovering deeper aspects *of established truth* and replacing foundational pillars with fundamentally different concepts. TFP maintains that the personality of God (the Father as a distinct, personal, tangible being, and Christ His literal Son), as understood and defended by the pioneers and Ellen White, *was* the established truth. The shift to Trinitarianism was not a Spirit-led progression but resulted from *"leaders whom the Lord has warned and entreated and counseled... choosing their own wisdom in the place of the wisdom of God"* (TFP, p. 313, quoting Ms137-1903.12), leading to a "new organization" built on a different foundation (TFP, p. 304).

The core difference remains hermeneutical: do we interpret Scripture and the testimonies according to their "obvious meaning," allowing terms like Father, Son, person, and form their plain sense, or do we adopt a "theological sense" (TFP, p. 273) that redefines these terms to fit a different paradigm? TFP stands firmly with the former, believing it aligns with the foundation God established.

We pray for continued dialogue, urging all, including esteemed scholars like Dr. Kaiser, to re-examine the historical evidence, especially the explicit warnings in the Spirit of Prophecy concerning the foundational principles and the dangers of departing from them. Let us return to *"the faith once delivered to the saints"* (TFP, p. 334).

Thank you.
