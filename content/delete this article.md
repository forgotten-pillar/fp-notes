## The Question Proposed

Some well meaning Adventists have asked us if the “Liberty of Conscience Petition” is an antri-trinitarianism in disguise? This is a question which prevents many to support this initiative, since they believe that this initiative would “legalized” anti-trinitarian heresy within the Seventh-day Adventist church.

The Liberty of Conscience petition has the sole purpose to exalt the Bible as the only authoritative test of faith. If this is being done and implemented in practice do anti-trinitarians have wide doors open in our church?

Let’s honestly ask ourselves. Can we test the personality of the Holy Spirit by the Bible? Absolutely! Can we test the full divinity of Christ by the Bible? Absolutely! Can we test Christ’s existence from all eternity by the Bible? Absolutely! Not only is the Bible positively testifying to these points, but are also strongly approved by the Spirit of Prophecy. The Bible is solely sufficient to guard the Seventh-day Adventist church against false views of the Holy Spirit, Christ, and God the Father, and the Spirit of Prophecy had stood shoulder to shoulder with the Scriptures in defense of the true view of God. But the benefit of the Sola Scripture principle is that the Bible is guarding the truth irrespective of someone's theological preferences, including those of Trinitarian one. Are there false views of God amongst us under the banner of “trinitarianism”? Unfortunately! But how should we test those views? By adherence to the 28 Fundamental Beliefs or by the bar of the full counsel of God’s Word?

Therefore, let’s test both of these views by the Scriptures, on each corresponding point of difference.
## Monotheism

The Fundamental Principles claim that monotheistic God of the Bible is only one being - the Father, and to Him the creation has been attributed.

The Fundamental Beliefs, on the other hand, claim that the monotheistic God of the Bible is a unity of three Persons, namely - the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

> [!bible] [1 Corinthians 8:4b, 6 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+8:4-6&version=kjv)
> <sup>**4**</sup> ...we know that an idol is nothing in the world, and that there is none other God but one.
> <sup>**6**</sup> But to us there is **but one God**, **the Father**, of **whom are  all things**, and we in him; and **one Lord Jesus Christ**, by whom are all things, and we by him.

The first two points of the Fundamental Principles are borrowing direct language from this verse, and the claim that "one God" - monotheistic God, is the Father. Our current Fundamental Beliefs does come to that conclusion, but if does adhere that the Father is "*the Creator, Source, Sustainer, and Sovereign of all creation*", as indicated that of the Father "*are all things*" - the Father is the source of all things.

Another verse about the monotheism is [John  17:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+17.3&version=KJV). Jesus was praying to His Father, and called him "*only true God*":

> [!bible] [John 17:3 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+17:3&version=kjv)
> <sup>**3**</sup> And this is life eternal, that they might know thee **the only true God**, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent.

There are other bible verses about monotheism like [Ephesians  4:4-6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+4&version=KJV) and [1. Timothy  2:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1.+Timothy+2.5&version=KJV), which directly speak of the Father as "one God". Also there no explicit verses in the Bible, which are describing the Son as the "*one God*", or the Holy Spirit to phrased in such way. But the Fundamental Beliefs come to the conclusion that "*one God*" is a unity of three person, through a specific theological synthesis, then direct voice of Scripture.

The theological reasoning for compromising the plain testimony of the Scripture that the Father it the monotheistic God of the Bible, stems from the adherence of the full divinity of Christ.

## Full divinity of Christ

Jesus is God, as stated in [John  1:1, 14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1&version=KJV); [20:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+20.28&version=KJV); [Hebrews  1:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+1.8&version=KJV); [Colossians  2:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Colossians+2.9&version=KJV); [Philippians  2:5-7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians+2&version=KJV); [Isaiah  9:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+9.6&version=KJV). Is this testimony of Scriptures contradictory of its own claim that the Father is "one God", yet here we see Another? It depends on the theological reasoning:

The Fundamental Beliefs has adjusted its view on the monotheism, to include Christ also as God. Is this particular theological reasoning the only way to harmonize seemingly contradictory claims? No! Fundamental Principles are proof to it.

The full divinity of Christ is upheld in the phrase "*the Son of the Eternal Father*". The question at hand is *how* is Jesus God? In the trinitarian view Jesus is the, or part of, the monotheistic God, while in non-trinitarian view Jesus is God by the merits of Sonship. It is due to acceptance the phrase "*the Son of God*", in the most obvious sense the Biblical language employs. Obviously children inherit the complete nature of their parents. So it is with Christ. Pauls says:

> [!bible] [Ephesians 3:14-15 - KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+3:14-15&version=kjv)
> <sup>**14**</sup> For this cause I bow my knees unto **the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ**,
> <sup>**15**</sup> Of whom the **whole family** [patria - fatherhood] in **heaven** and **earth is named**,

The trinitarian view does not take the Fatherhood of God, nor Sonship of Christ literally, rather limits the Father and the Son relationship only to the scope of functional roles within the Plan of Salvation.

> “The sonship of Jesus, however, **is not ontological but functional**. In the plan of salvation each member of the Trinity has accepted **a particular role**. It is a role for the purpose of accomplishing a particular goal, not a change in essence or status.” [{Gerhard Pfandl, Biblical Research Institute, THE TRINITY IN SCRIPTURE, June 1999.}](https://www.adventistbiblicalresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/trinscript.pdf)

> “Sonship **is not His innate, eternal identity**, but rather **a role** He took up for a purpose.” {Ty Gipson, The Sonship of Christ, p. 72 Kindle}

> “But all of this meaningful and beautiful gospel theology is lost if we push the Sonship of Christ off into some unique identity that He alone possesses from eternity past. None of Paul’s narrative logic makes any sense if we work from the premise that **Jesus is God’s Son in an ancient, ontological sense**.” {Ty Gipson, The Sonship of Christ, p. 78 Kindle}

The word 'ontological' employed by theologians refers to a literal Sonship of Christ, counteracting the obvious meaning of words "the Son of God". The reason why such obvious meaning employed by the Scriptures is denied, because that would logically imply that Jesus is not eternal:

> “...the father-son image **cannot be literally** applied to the divine Father-Son relationship within the Godhead. **The Son is not the natural, literal Son of the Father**. A natural child **has a beginning**, while within the Godhead the Son is eternal. The term ‘Son’ is used **metaphorically** when applied to the Godhead.” [{BRI, Question on Sonship}](https://www.adventistbiblicalresearch.org/materials/a-question-of-sonship/)

We can see a logical trail of theological reasoning, which starts with biblical truth that Jesus is eternal, therefore - this reasoning concludes, the Sonship of Christ, nor Fatherhood of God is accepted in an obvious sense, leading to final conclusion that Christ's full divinity is affirm by idea of one God as unity of three persons, crossing out the obvious language of Scriptures that the  monotheistic God is Father. But we have to have in mind that this is a theological reasoning and synthesis. We are free to test it by the Scriptures!

The question of "how" is God one and yet three, is generally deemed as a mystery, not revealed in the Scriptures, mainly because it touches on God's nature. We are finite, and God is infinite. But, can we use the same force of the argument that Christ is eternal ([Micah  5:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Micha+5.2&version=KJV), [Proverbs  8:23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+8.23&version=KJV)), and yet accept the obvious language of Inspiration that Christ is also only begotten Son of God - "*begotten in the express image of the Father's person*" [{ST May 30, 1895, par. 3}](https://egwwritings.org/?ref=enST.May.30.1895.par.3&para=820.12891)? We are finite and we cannot grasp the plain language of the Scriptures that His "***goings forth** have been from of old, **from everlasting***" ([Micah  5:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Micah+5.2&version=KJV)), and that He "*was **set up** from **everlasting**, from the **beginning***" ([Proverbs  8:23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+8.23&version=KJV)). To our finite minds, this language is truly illogical and contradicting: how can He from everlasting and yet be begotten? If there is some seemingly contradicting notion, caused by our finite comprehension, let it rests with the Scriptures, then with our theological reasoning.

If we accept by faith that Christ is eternal and yet truly begotten of the Father, just as trinitarian paradigm accepts the mystery of one God in three Persons, then we would have a case for Christ's full divinity based on His Sonship, accepted and explained in the simple language of the Scriptures, and would preserve plain and direct testimony of Christ, that His Father is "*the only true God*."

In the context of this experiment, if we have two statements of faith, which one is more Biblically sound?
1) **God is**: a unity of three persons - the Father, the Son and the Holy Ghost
2) **God is**: the Father of Christ, and Christ is the Son of God

If our test would only be 28 Fundamental Beliefs we would never examine it by the Scriptures in the first place. But we see that the Scripture is sufficient to teach simple monotheism, Christ's full divinity and His eternal existence.

## The personality of the Holy Spirit

The Scriptures provide full sufficiency to teach this truth. The personality of the Holy Spirit is revealed in His functional work: The Holy Spirit speaks ([Acts  13:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+13.2&version=KJV)), teaches ([John  14:26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+14.26&version=KJV)), makes decisions ([Acts  15:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+15.28&version=KJV)), can be grieved ([Ephesians  4:30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+4.30&version=KJV)), can be lied to ([Acts  5:3,4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+5.3&version=KJV)), can forbid or prevent human speech and plans ([Acts  16:6,7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+16.6&version=KJV)), searches everything and comprehends God’s thoughts ([1 Corinthians  2:10,11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+2.10&version=KJV)), and bears witness ([Romans  8:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+8.16&version=KJV); [John  15:26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+15.26&version=KJV)), and others.

The council of Ellen White is in full agreement with this reasoning:

> **The Holy Spirit has a personality**, *else* **He could not bear witness to our spirits** and with our spirits that we are the children of God. **He must also be a divine person**, *else* **He could not search out the secrets** which lie hidden in the mind of God. "For what man knoweth the things of a man save the spirit of man, which is in him; even so the things of God knoweth no man, but the Spirit of God." [1 Corinthians 2:11.]. [{EGW; 21LtMs, Ms 20, 1906, par. 32}](https://egwwritings.org/read?panels=p14071.10296041&index=0)

Our Fundamental Beliefs are fully in agreement with clear Testimonies of the Scriptures.

The Fundamental Principles uplifted the personality of the Holy Spirit in its expression that the Holy Spirit is an active 'Representative' of God. How can a Representative be impersonal? God is "*everywhere present by **His** representative the Holy Spirit*." It is an active representative, a means by which God is everywhere present, the Holy Spirit bares witness to our spirits. He acts in God's stead.

Both statements adhere to the personality of the Holy Spirit, it is just that the Fundamental Beliefs are more explicit about it.

## Omnipresence of God

Here we see difference between both statements:

In Fundamental Beliefs God is omnipresent by Himself. That God is a unity of three persons.

In Fundamental Principles God is not omnipresent by Himself. His omnipresence is achieved through "*His representative the Holy Spirit.*" Again, the God we are talking about is the Father specifically.

This might not be obvious difference, but it is of huge significance. The theological repercussions are startling in the light of the personality of God.

## The personality of God

The personality of God deals with the question on *how* is God a person. In particular *how* is the Father person. We have seen *how* is the Holy Spirit a person, obviously the Word of God is revealing this to us.

In the #5 of the FB, of the Holy Spirit is spoken: "***He is as much a person as are the Father and the Son***". This suggests that the Father and the Son are persons in the same sense as the Holy Spirit is a person: They speak, teach, make decisions, bear witness etc. In another words, as much as the Holy Spirit is a person in a functional way, so are the Father and the Son persons in a functional way. The Father and the Son are not persons in ontological way, but only functional only. In the context of the Trinity we are not allowed to talk about ontological aspect of God, since this is a mystery.

But we ask a legitimate question: "*how*" is the Father a person? Does the Bible give a clear answer to that question? If you are avid researched of Ellen White, you would know that Ellen White talked a lot about the personality of God. She had a vision on it, and it reveals the answer to the question "how" is the Father a person:

> "I have often **seen** the lovely **Jesus**, that **He is a person**. I asked **Him if His Father *was a person***, and **had *a form* like Himself**. Said Jesus, 'I am **the express image of My Father's person**!' [Hebrews 1:3.]. [{EGW; 18LtMs, Lt 253, 1903, par. 12}](https://egwwritings.org/read?panels=p14068.9980018&index=0)

What makes the Father a person? Is it His ability to speak, bear witness and similar qualities? It was particularly questioned about the outward visible **form** of God. In another words, God the Father is a person because He posses material, tangible form of a person.

As such, He is located in a specific location: In Heaven, in Heavenly Sanctuary, where He sits and rules from His Throne. Consequently we are led to clear Adventist doctrine, that Heaven is a real place ([John  14:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+14.2&version=KJV)), the Heavenly Sanctuary is real and "*the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and not man*" ([Hebrews  8:2,3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+8.2&version=KJV)). But though He personally dwells in Heaven, He is everywhere present by His Representative the Holy Spirit. Therefore inferring from a simplicity of Bible's testimony, the Father and the Son are ontologically persons (They have each distinct material forms), while the Holy Spirit is a person, strictly in a functional sense, possessing no outward visible form of a person ([Luke  24:39](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+24.39&version=KJV)).

Ellen White as well as many canonical prophets have been in the presence of God, and had seen the glory of God's person. The question come to the interpretation of these accounts. Should we accept the presented view of God in their obvious sense, or in some spiritual sense? Right after recalling her vision she said:

> "I have often seen that **the spiritual view** took away all the glory of heaven, and that in many minds the throne of David and the lovely person of Jesus have been burned up in the **fire of Spiritualism**. I have seen that some who have been deceived and led into this error will be brought out into the light of truth, but it will be almost impossible for them to get entirely rid of **the deceptive power of Spiritualism**" [{EGW; Lt 253, 1903, par. 13.}](https://egwwritings.org/read?panels=p28.490(28.491)&index=0)

The reason why lovely person of Jesus and God's Throne are burned in the fire of spiritualism is a direct result of a spiritual view of God's person. The question on the personality of God is not a question of hermeneutical preference, since we have a direct revelation that the Scriptures presentation of who and what God is must be accepted in its plain language.

If we accept the trinitarian notion that the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit are three co-equal persons, persons in the exact same way or sense, and we rightfully affirm that personality of the Holy Spirit to be a person in a functional sense, then consequently we are devoting the Father and the Son of their visible material forms, ending in the realms of spiritualism. We know that such view is an error, and yet our current Fundamental Beliefs statement not only that lacks guard rails against that error, yet it leaves the place to support such view of God.

The Fundamental Principles, on the other hand, adhere the doctrine on the personality of God, by stating that "*one God*" - the Father, is "*a personal spiritual being*", everywhere present by His Representative the Holy Spirit. This language describes the intricate connection between Bible's clear teaching on the presence and the personality of God.
## Conclusion on the Personality of God

With the doctrine on the presence and the personality of God, we have made the full circle: this doctrine if accepted in its simplicity affirms Christ's Sonship, it affirms the Father as a monotheistic God of the Bible, and it affirms the personality of the Holy Spirit. This doctrine had a full standing in the beginning of our church and was firmly present throughout the life of Ellen White, especially in her final decade of life when she was combating Kellogg's error with it.

Adventist faith has a two mutually exclusive doctrines dealing with the view of God: The Trinity doctrine, and the doctrine on the presence and the personality of God. The difference is seen in the interpretation of the Bible language. It is either that God has adjusted Himself to us, explaining Himself in metaphors of like "Father", "Son", "Spirit", accepting those roles, while in reality, outside of the Plan of Salvation, God is **not** so. Contrary to that interpretation, God has adjusted man to Himself, He created man in His own image, where terms like "Father", "Son" and "Spirit" as read in the obvious meaning language employs, admitting innate reality of God beyond the glorious Plan of Salvation.

The contrast is stark. One is view holds complicated theological synthesis vailing the plain language of the Bible in symbolism, while other accepts the simple and apparent language of the Bible. Both views do have elements which are incomprehensible by the finite human mind, so they are being accepted by faith. Respectively, both of them adhere to the full divinity of Christ, and the personality of the Holy Spirit. Yet with the Scripture in our hands we are able to determine which one is closer, more coherent and clear.
